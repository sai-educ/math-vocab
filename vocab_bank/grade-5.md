# Grade 5 Math Vocabulary


## Operations & Algebraic Thinking (5.OA)

### order of operations

**Definition:** The agreed order for working a problem. Do parentheses first. Then multiply and divide. Add and subtract last.

**Example:** Theo solves 2 + 3 × 4. He multiplies first and gets 12. Then he adds 2 for a total of 14.

**Common misconception:** Working left to right gives 20 here, which is wrong. Multiplying comes before adding.

**Standards:** US 5.OA

### parentheses

**Definition:** Curved marks that show what to work out first.

**Example:** Nina solves (2 + 3) × 4. The parentheses come first, so she gets 5. Then 5 × 4 gives 20.

**Standards:** US 5.OA

### expression

**Definition:** A math phrase with numbers and signs. It has no equal sign.

**Example:** Omar writes 6 × (2 + 1). That is an expression, not an equation.

**Standards:** US 5.OA

### evaluate

**Definition:** Work out what an expression is worth.

**Example:** Maya evaluates 4 × (3 + 2). Inside the parentheses gives 5. Then 4 × 5 gives 20.

**Standards:** US 5.OA

### numerical pattern

**Definition:** A list of numbers that follows a rule.

**Example:** Leo starts at 3 and adds 5 each time: 3, 8, 13, 18. His pattern grows by 5.

**Standards:** US 5.OA

### coordinate pair

**Definition:** Two numbers that locate a point on a grid.

**Example:** Ana plots (4, 3). She moves 4 across, then 3 up, and marks the spot.

**Standards:** US 5.OA

### square number

**Definition:** A number made by multiplying a whole number by itself.

**Example:** Zoe multiplies 6 x 6 and gets 36. 36 is a square number.

**Standards:** UK Y5 glossary, IN Class V · 5.M.LO7

### cube number

**Definition:** A number made by multiplying a whole number by itself three times.

**Example:** Theo multiplies 3 x 3 x 3 and gets 27. 27 is a cube number.

**Standards:** UK Y5 glossary

### triangular number

**Definition:** A number that can be arranged as dots in a triangle, like 1, 3, 6, 10.

**Example:** Nina stacks dots in rows of 1, then 2, then 3. The running totals, 1, 3, 6, are triangular numbers.

**Standards:** UK glossary, IN Class V · 5.M.LO7


## Number & Operations in Base Ten (5.NBT)

### exponent

**Definition:** A small raised number. It tells how many times to multiply the base by itself.

**Example:** Ben writes 10 with a small 3 above it. The exponent means 10 × 10 × 10, which is 1,000.

**Common misconception:** An exponent is not the same as multiplying by 3. It means three tens multiplied together.

**Standards:** US 5.NBT

### power of ten

**Definition:** What you get when you multiply 10 by itself.

**Example:** Ivy sees that 100 is 10 × 10. That makes 100 a power of ten.

**Standards:** US 5.NBT

### round decimals

**Definition:** Change a decimal to a nearby simpler one.

**Example:** Zoe rounds 3.67 to the nearest tenth. The 7 pushes it up, so she writes 3.7.

**Standards:** US 5.NBT

### multiply decimals

**Definition:** Multiply as you would whole numbers. Then place the dot.

**Example:** Theo multiplies 0.4 × 0.3. He thinks 4 × 3 = 12. Two decimal places give him 0.12.

**Common misconception:** The answer can be smaller than what you started with. Multiplying by a part shrinks it.

**Standards:** US 5.NBT

### divide decimals

**Definition:** Split a decimal into equal parts.

**Example:** Nina pours 4.5 liters into 3 bottles evenly. Each bottle gets 1.5 liters.

**Standards:** US 5.NBT

### thousandths

**Definition:** A place value equal to one-thousandth of a whole. It is the third digit after the decimal point.

**Example:** Omar writes 0.125. The 5 is in the thousandths place.

**Standards:** US 5.NBT.A.1, UK Y5

### lakh

**Definition:** An Indian place-value unit equal to 100,000.

**Example:** Maya reads that a car costs 5 lakh rupees, which means 500,000 rupees.

**Standards:** IN Class V · 5.M.LO1.1

### crore

**Definition:** An Indian place-value unit equal to 10,000,000.

**Example:** Leo reads that a stadium cost 50 crore rupees to build, which means 500,000,000 rupees.

**Standards:** IN Class V · 5.M.LO1.1


## Number & Operations—Fractions (5.NF)

### common denominator

**Definition:** A shared bottom number that lets you add or compare fractions.

**Example:** Omar adds 1/2 and 1/3. He rewrites them as 3/6 and 2/6, then adds to get 5/6.

**Common misconception:** You cannot add the bottom numbers. Make them match first, then add only the tops.

**Standards:** US 5.NF

### mixed number

**Definition:** A whole number plus a fraction.

**Example:** Maya runs 3 whole laps and half of another. She ran 3 1/2 laps.

**Standards:** US 5.NF

### multiply fractions

**Definition:** Multiply the top numbers. Then multiply the bottom numbers.

**Example:** Leo scoops 1/2 of a 1/3 cup measure. He multiplies and finds he has 1/6 of a cup.

**Standards:** US 5.NF

### divide fractions

**Definition:** Split a fraction into equal parts, or find how many fit inside.

**Example:** Ana has 1/2 a pizza and shares it with 2 people. Each one gets 1/4 of the pizza.

**Standards:** US 5.NF

### simplify

**Definition:** Write a fraction with the smallest numbers that still name the same amount.

**Example:** Ben has 6/8 of a bar. He divides the top and bottom by 2 and writes 3/4.

**Standards:** US 5.NF

### reciprocal

**Definition:** The fraction you get when you flip one over.

**Example:** Ivy flips 2/5 and gets 5/2. That is the reciprocal. The two of them multiply to 1.

**Standards:** US 5.NF

### percentage

**Definition:** A fraction expressed as parts per hundred, written with a % sign.

**Example:** Ana scores 90% on her spelling test. Out of 100 equal parts, she got 90 of them right.

**Standards:** UK Y5 programme of study


## Measurement & Data (5.MD)

### volume

**Definition:** The amount of space a solid takes up.

**Example:** Zoe fills a box with unit cubes. It holds 3 layers of 8, so the volume is 24 cubic units.

**Common misconception:** Volume fills the inside. Area only covers a flat surface.

**Standards:** US 5.MD

### cubic unit

**Definition:** One cube used to measure volume.

**Example:** Theo stacks cubes inside a box with no gaps. Each cube is one cubic unit, and he counts 18.

**Standards:** US 5.MD

### convert units

**Definition:** Change a measure to a different unit.

**Example:** Nina has 2,000 meters left to run. She converts it and sees that is just 2 kilometers.

**Standards:** US 5.MD

### knot

**Definition:** A unit used to measure the speed of a boat or the wind. It is not a unit of length.

**Example:** Omar reads that a ship is sailing at 12 knots, meaning it covers 12 nautical miles every hour.

**Common misconception:** A knot already means a speed, not a distance. Nobody says 'knots per hour'.

**Standards:** US 5.MD

### coordinate plane

**Definition:** A grid made by two number lines that cross.

**Example:** Omar maps his neighborhood on a coordinate plane. He finds the park at (4, 3).

**Standards:** US 5.MD

### x-axis

**Definition:** The number line that runs across the grid.

**Example:** Maya moves right along the x-axis to 5 before she moves up at all.

**Standards:** US 5.MD

### y-axis

**Definition:** The number line that runs up and down the grid.

**Example:** Leo counts up the y-axis to 4 to finish plotting his point.

**Standards:** US 5.MD


## Geometry (5.G)

### coordinate plane

**Definition:** A grid for locating points. A level line and an upright line cross to make it.

**Example:** Ana plots (4, 3) on the coordinate plane. She goes 4 across, then 3 up.

**Standards:** US 5.G

### ordered pair

**Definition:** Two numbers in order that give a point's spot. The across number comes first.

**Example:** Ben plots (2, 5). He moves 2 across, then 5 up. The order changes where he lands.

**Common misconception:** Swapping the numbers lands you somewhere else. (2, 5) is not the same as (5, 2).

**Standards:** US 5.G

### classify

**Definition:** Sort shapes into groups by what they have in common.

**Example:** Ivy classifies triangles by their angles. She makes one pile each for acute, right, and obtuse.

**Standards:** US 5.G

### hierarchy of shapes

**Definition:** A way to order shape groups. Smaller groups fit inside bigger ones.

**Example:** Zoe draws a chart of shapes. Squares fit inside rectangles, and rectangles fit inside quadrilaterals.

**Common misconception:** Every square is a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square.

**Standards:** US 5.G

### quadrant

**Definition:** One of the four sections made when a coordinate plane is divided by its axes.

**Example:** Theo plots a point with two positive coordinates. It lands in the first quadrant.

**Standards:** US 5.G.A.1, UK Y4

### rotation symmetry

**Definition:** When a shape looks the same after being turned part of the way around its center.

**Example:** Omar spins a pinwheel shape a third of the way around. It looks exactly the same, so it has rotation symmetry.

**Standards:** IN Class V · 5.M.LO3.2, UK glossary

### regular polygon

**Definition:** A polygon where all the sides and all the angles are equal.

**Example:** Maya checks a hexagon tile. All the sides and all the angles match, so it is a regular polygon.

**Standards:** UK Y5 programme of study

### irregular polygon

**Definition:** A polygon where the sides and angles are not all equal.

**Example:** Leo draws a five-sided shape. Its sides are not all the same length, so it is an irregular polygon.

**Standards:** UK Y5 programme of study

### reflex angle

**Definition:** An angle greater than 180 degrees but less than 360 degrees.

**Example:** A door swings almost all the way open. Ana measures the angle: 300 degrees. That is a reflex angle.

**Common misconception:** Kids often measure the small angle instead of the wide one. A reflex angle is always the bigger way around.

**Standards:** UK Y5 programme of study
